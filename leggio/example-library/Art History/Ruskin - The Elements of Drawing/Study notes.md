# The Elements of Drawing. Study notes

John Ruskin, 1857. A book of letters to a beginner, written to teach seeing before drawing.

## The innocent eye

Ruskin asks the reader to look at the world as flat patches of color, the way a person who had suddenly gained sight would see it. Shadows are not black holes and a green field is not one green: drawing well starts from noticing what is really there, not what we know is there.

## The first exercises

The first exercises are slow on purpose. Fill a square with even shading, then a gradation from dark to light, then copy the outline of a leaf or a stone. Each one trains the hand to obey the eye.

## Why it still matters

The book is less about technique than about attention. Modern design schools repeat the same idea in other words: look longer, draw less, and correct what you see, not what you expected.

**In one sentence:** for Ruskin, drawing is a way of learning to see, and the exercises exist to slow the eye down.
